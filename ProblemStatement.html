NYC Transit Desert Solutions

Overview

New York City has extensive transit, yet transit deserts remain in outer boroughs like Queens, Bronx, Brooklyn, and Staten Island. This page explores solutions to improve transit accessibility.

Problem Statement

  • Summary: Many people lack access to reliable public transportation. They have difficulty in commuting to their destinations.
  • Context: Poorly planned transit infrastructure contributes to gaps in public transportation services. Low density neighborhoods are generally less served by a transit network.
  • Impact: Impoverished people and the Nonwhites tend to disproportionally locate themselves in transit deserts, and are not inclined to buy a personal automobile.
  • Root Causes: Disinvestment from transit deserts disincentivizes expansion of service into such areas, as transit agencies and local governments lack funding to undertake such projects.
  • Outcome: In ascending order, these outcomes address the problem in a more fundamental manner: A mitigation includes expanding transit networks and improving service frequency. A remedy includes investment in microtransit. A solution includes transit-oriented development to build communities that are fundamentally centered around public transportation.

Implementation Timeline

  • 0-3 Years: Improve bus service, microtransit, bike infrastructure.
  • 3-7 Years: Develop BRT corridors, expand ferry service, zoning incentives.
  • 7+ Years: Complete Interborough Express, expand subway connections.
